# Marketing / Landing UI

For landing pages, pricing pages, portfolios, product marketing sites — pages meant to persuade a visitor who's seeing this for the first time, in the first few seconds. The bar here is different from product UI: a strong, specific first impression matters more than exhaustive coverage of every feature.

## The hero is doing most of the work

The hero section should communicate what the thing is and who it's for without requiring a scroll. Avoid generic headline patterns that could describe any product ("The future of X," "Do more with less") — a headline specific enough that it couldn't be pasted onto a competitor's site is doing its job. One clear primary call-to-action beats three competing ones.

## Structure and pacing

A landing page tells a story: problem/context → what this is → why it's credible → what it costs/how to start. Vary section rhythm (a full-bleed visual section, then a dense text section, then a grid) rather than repeating the same "icon, headline, paragraph" card pattern for every section — that repetition is one of the clearest AI-generated tells, especially in 3-column feature grids where every card has identical structure and near-identical copy length.

## Social proof and credibility

Real specifics (a number, a named customer, an actual quote) read as credible; vague claims ("trusted by thousands") read as filler. If there's no real social proof yet, it's better to omit the section than fill it with placeholder logos or fabricated quotes.

## Visual identity

This is the one place where a bolder, more opinionated aesthetic is appropriate — marketing pages are allowed to have more visual personality than a dashboard. That said, "bold" should still come from a deliberate choice (a strong color, an unusual layout, real photography/illustration) rather than from stacking every trendy effect (gradient + glassmorphism + floating blobs + gratuitous scroll animation) at once, which reads as generic rather than bold.

## Conversion details

CTA buttons should be visually consistent across the page (same primary color/style every time the same action is offered). Forms should be short — every field is friction. If there's pricing, make the recommended/most-popular tier visually distinct, but don't over-decorate it to the point it looks like a coupon ad.
